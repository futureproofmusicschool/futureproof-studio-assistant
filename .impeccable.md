## Design Context

### Users

Music producers working at a desktop studio setup. They use the product as a persistent collaborator while writing, arranging, researching, organizing projects, and controlling Ableton Live. The primary job is to keep creative work moving toward finished tracks without losing context between conversations.

### Brand Personality

Focused, opinionated, and premium. The interface should feel like a trusted studio instrument: calm enough for long sessions, direct enough for decisive work, and alive when it is listening or acting. It should never feel like a generic chatbot, project-management template, or novelty AI demo.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark editorial studio console. Use tinted graphite surfaces, warm off-white typography, restrained Futureproof purple as an operational signal, Panchang for short display statements, and Satoshi for the working interface. Prefer rules, rhythm, transcript structure, and purposeful signal motion over card grids, gradients, glass effects, glows, or decorative dashboards.

### Design Principles

1. Conversation is the main instrument; status supports it without competing with it.
2. Show real capability and connection state. Never invent progress percentages or decorative green lights.
3. Make the product promise legible in one glance: this studio partner carries the work forward and remembers it.
4. Use purple only to communicate activity, selection, focus, or a live connection.
5. Public-facing states must use generic content and hide personal conversation, contacts, and studio information.
6. Motion should feel like a quiet audio signal and must respect reduced-motion preferences.
